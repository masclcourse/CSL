6-week study plan

About 6–10 hours a week. Adjust to your schedule and experience.

WK 1

Get the books & the bulletin

Read the candidate bulletin, order the exact editions on the list, and tab every book with a first pass through each table of contents.

WK 2

Business & law

Licensing law (M.G.L. c.143), Home Improvement Contractor law (c.142A), mechanics liens (c.254), contracts, insurance and worker rules.

WK 3

Building code, part 1

Use and occupancy, construction types, egress, fire protection. Practice finding answers fast, not memorizing.

WK 4

Building code, part 2

Structure, framing, foundations, energy code, accessibility, and the residential sections.

WK 5

Safety & plans

OSHA construction standards (29 CFR 1926), site safety, reading plans and specs, estimating basics.

WK 6

Timed practice

Full timed practice exams with only your tabbed books. Review every miss and add tabs for what slowed you down.

Open-book strategy

Open book does not mean easy. You pass by finding the right page in under two minutes.

  • Tab every chapter and every table you expect to use. Color-code by book.
  • Write the page number of the most common tables on the inside cover.
  • Use the index and the table of contents on practice questions until the search becomes automatic.
  • Only bring the editions listed in the current bulletin. A wrong edition can cost you points or be refused.
  • Highlighting and tabs are usually fine, but loose notes may not be. Check the bulletin's rules on what can be written in books.

Exam day

Remove every avoidable problem.

  • Bring valid ID matching your registration name exactly, plus your confirmation.
  • Arrive early. Phones, tablets and laptops cannot stand in for printed books.
  • Answer the questions you know first, mark the slow ones, and come back.
  • Do not leave blanks. There is no reason to skip an answer.
  • If you pass, keep your score report. It is needed for the license application.
